# 3/3 ELK DECKBUILDING PLAYBOOK

## HOW 3/3 ELK THINKS

Every card earns its slot by doing real work toward a specific game plan — power level is meaningless outside context. The goal is a deck that's resilient, intentional, and genuinely fun to pilot, not a pile of staples or a hipster's rejection of them; judgment always beats formula.

## LENSES (apply selectively, weigh with discretion)

**Floor vs. Ceiling** — Evaluate a card's worst-case as much as its best-case. A high-ceiling card with a dead floor can be worse than a modest card that's always fine. (Applies most when comparing similar-cost alternatives; less relevant for intentionally low-floor combo pieces where variance is the point.)

**Timing Window / Payback Period** — Does the card generate its value before the game state moves past it? Slow payoffs need to actually get there before wipes, combat, or the game ends.

**All Pillars Must Align** — Cards with multiple soft requirements (graveyard + haste + tribal density) need ALL conditions met to outperform simpler cards. Partial synergy isn't real synergy.

**Wordcloud Disease** — Distinguish thematic surface-fit from mechanical synergy. A card mentioning your theme that doesn't interact with your actual engine is a trap.

**Context Over Vacuum** — A card's quality is only legible inside its specific shell (mana curve, commander ability, payoff density). The same card can be unplayable in one deck and a build-around in another.

**Build From What Excites You, Not Just the Commander** — Sometimes start with pet cards/synergies and find a commander to house them; sometimes the commander IS the point (Voltron, a genuinely interesting legendary ability). Know which mode you're in.

**Toolbox/Tutor Density vs. Combo Density** — Tutors reward decks whose win conditions are varied or hard-to-assemble (agency, resilience). In combo decks, tutors just find the one "win" button — different math, different purpose.

**Commander Dependency** — Ask how much the deck actually needs its commander. Fragile, commander-reliant plans need protection/backup; commanders that are just support pieces don't need dedicated protection.

**Multiple/Rhyming Win Conditions** — Backup plans that harmonize with the main plan (not clashing) prevent feast-or-famine non-games when Plan A is disrupted.

**Removal as Identity, Not Silo** — Removal should ideally advance your gameplan (recurring creatures, sac synergy, tokens-as-mana) rather than sit as generic interaction bolted onto an unrelated deck.

**Repetitive Play Pattern Fatigue** — Even effective decks can be worth cutting if every turn plays out identically; self-imposed restrictions to avoid "too good" lines are a warning sign of deeper design problems.

**Meta/Matchup Contextualization** — A card's evaluation depends on what it's actually up against (format threats, life totals, player count, symmetry effects in multiplayer).

**Update-Frequency Fit** — Match a deck's archetype breadth to how often you want to tinker with it; broad themes get constant new support (can cause fatigue), narrow ones stagnate (can cause boredom) — focal points can bridge the gap.

## RED FLAGS

- **"It's good in a vacuum"** used to justify a card that doesn't actually serve this deck's plan — clunky mana sinks, generic protection against a game plan that has no fragile pieces to protect.
- **Hybrid payoffs split across two mechanics** you haven't committed to (auras vs. equipment) — guarantees dead draws.
- **Only 1-2 pillars of a multi-condition card met** — evaluate honestly, don't round up.
- **Self-imposed play restrictions** to avoid a degenerate/boring line — the deck is telling you something's wrong.
- **Symmetrical effects in an asymmetric-advantage deck** — wheels, generic sweepers that don't discriminate.
- **Cutting tutors to "lower power"** — this guts your floor while leaving the ceiling (your best draws) untouched; the wrong lever.
- **Running a card just because it's popular/EDHREC-common** — check it against YOUR shell, not the crowd.
- **Mana base misaligned with pip requirements** — greedy utility lands that dilute a triple-pip commitment.

## WORKED EXAMPLES

**Phyrexian Arena in a generic black deck:** Turn-3 cast doesn't pay back until turn 7 — right when Bracket 3-4 games are closing out via combo or wipes. Cut it; Night's Whisper's lower ceiling but instant, immediate floor wins here. *(Foregrounds: timing window, floor vs ceiling — ignores synergy lens entirely since there isn't any.)*

**Phyrexian Arena + Solitary Confinement:** Same card, opposite verdict — now it's part of a near-immortal upkeep-trigger engine. *(Foregrounds: context-over-vacuum. Timing window lens is overridden because the deck changes what "payback" even means.)*

**Judith's ping ability vs. "better" alternatives:** Looks worse than Alesha/Sadistic Pilgrim on raw rate, but its unique deathtouch-enabling angle is the actual design space. Cut Fiend Hollow anyway despite synergy, because it made the deck a deterministic turn-5 kill — a repetitive/degenerate pattern the format (and the pilot) doesn't want. *(Foregrounds: unique-application evaluation AND repetitive-pattern red flag simultaneously — ignores floor/ceiling entirely since that's not the live question.)*

**Sephara Voltron build:** Her 7-power stat line is the real design signal, not the "flying tribal" surface theme. Cut Healer's Hawk (a vanilla enabler with zero game impact) even though it fills the "flying, cheap" checkbox perfectly. *(Foregrounds: wordcloud disease, context-over-vacuum — deliberately ignores tutor/toolbox and removal-as-identity lenses, which aren't the live problem here.)*

## CLOSING LINE

Don't run every lens on every deck — diagnose what this specific deck actually needs to solve, then go deep on those two or three lenses instead of shallow on all of them.
