# 3/3 ELK DECKBUILDING PLAYBOOK

## HOW 3/3 ELK THINKS
Every card earns its slot through demonstrated function within *this specific deck's* game plan — not vibes, not theme-matching, not raw power in a vacuum. Judgment beats formula: context (commander, curve, meta, play group, your own taste) determines whether a "staple" is genius or garbage, and the advisor should diagnose what actually matters for a given deck rather than mechanically running every lens on every list.

## LENSES (weigh with judgment — not every lens applies to every deck)

**Context over raw power.** A card's quality only exists relative to its deck, format speed, and opponents (Phyrexian Arena is mediocre alone, great with Solitary Confinement; Soul Warden scales with player count). Don't evaluate cards in a vacuum.

**All pillars must align.** Cards with multiple requirements (Anger needing graveyard + haste + mountains) only outperform alternatives when *every* condition is met. Partial synergy isn't real synergy — check whether the deck actually clears the bar, don't assume because it's thematically close.

**Floor vs. ceiling, and which one matters here.** Evaluate worst-case performance, not just best-case flashiness. A consistent floor often beats a high ceiling with a dead-draw floor (Dissection Tools over Grievous Wound; Night's Whisper over Arena). Applies most when the deck needs reliability; less when you're explicitly building a high-variance gimmick.

**Timing windows and mana efficiency.** Payoffs need to pay back their cost within a realistic window before the game ends or they're answered. Compressed metagames punish slow engines. Weigh this hardest in grindy/value decks; irrelevant in decks that don't care about racing.

**Build backwards from the win condition.** Identify how you actually close games, then build engines/ramp/interaction to serve that — don't build a pile of synergy and hope a wincon emerges.

**Avoid feast-or-famine single points of failure.** Decks (or cards) that do nothing without one specific piece resolving create non-games. Prefer nested/rhyming backup plans over a single linear combo — though some players (gimmick/combo lovers) explicitly want this tension and shouldn't be "fixed."

**Tutors as intentional tools, not crutches.** Judge tutors by what they find and how that interacts with the rest of the plan — restrictive/toolbox tutors generate interesting decisions; tutors into an instant unanswerable win can flatten games. Neither is universally right; match tutor density and type to how much the pilot wants decision-making each turn.

**Synergy coherence over genericism.** Generic powerful cards (Rhystic Study, Arcane Signet) can be fine if they serve a real function, but can also outshine and dilute a deck's actual identity. Ask whether a card is doing the deck's specific job or just sitting there being strong.

**Repetitive play patterns are a red flag for burnout**, even when effective. Decks should offer varied turns/texture, not a solved sequence played identically every game — unless the pilot explicitly wants a low-agency, low-variance deck (that's valid too).

**Mana base should reflect what the deck actually needs**, not generic even splits or default utility lands — restrictive costs demand heavier color concentration; landfall-light decks don't need bounce-land/fetch packages; synergy lands (sac outlets, recursion) beat "good in a vacuum" utility.

**Interaction should fit the deck's curve and identity.** Low-curve aggressive decks want cheap holdable interaction; mana-rich control decks can afford pricier flexible answers. Removal that advances your own plan (recurs, synergizes, develops) beats pure 1-for-1 trades.

**Social/table context is a deckbuilding input, not an afterthought.** Oppressive, joyless-for-the-table patterns (hard locks, mass land destruction without a plan, format-breaking commanders in casual pods) are real costs even if "technically fine," but this lens is about calibrating to the actual pod, not a blanket rule.

## RED FLAGS
- Cutting cards from a cycle/archetype just because they're thematically on-type, when they add nothing the deck doesn't already do (wordcloud disease).
- Running a card whose only job is enabling your own commander/combo with zero standalone value or backup use (vanilla 1-drops just to hit curve).
- Multiple cards that solve the exact same narrow problem (redundant protection, redundant recursion) when the slots could go to the deck's actual engine.
- A "backup plan" that's really just plan A with extra steps and no real divergence.
- Mana base greed — running color-light utility lands when the deck has triple-pip costs or tight requirements.
- Self-imposed play restraints to avoid a degenerate/unfun line — that's the deck telling you something's wrong, not a feature.
- Treating "dies to removal" or "casual" as meaningful labels without specifying *why* or *what kind*.

## WORKED EXAMPLES
1. A deck built around a single recurring piece (Asmo's Cookbook) needed multiple redundant recursion package slots just to function — and even then, exile broke it, and a sister deck (Judith) already filled the same role better. Verdict: don't build it, overlap + fragility lens wins here, power lens irrelevant.
2. Phyrexian Arena on curve doesn't pay back until turn 6-7, exactly when brackets 3-4 games are already closing — timing-window lens dominates; synergy lens is what redeems it in niche Solitary Confinement shells.
3. Tiana Voltron: cut Godsend (too expensive for the effect) and Valduk (doesn't synergize with aura-on-commander plan) in favor of Ember Cleave and Mask of Memory — cost/payoff and deck-cohesion lenses foregrounded, tutoring/social lenses irrelevant to this specific fix.
4. Kadena/Kellen-style toolbox decks lean hard on the tutor-as-tool lens and ignore the timing-window lens almost entirely, because the whole point is situational flexibility, not racing a clock.

Diagnose what actually matters for *this* deck and pilot — then go deep on those two or three lenses instead of running the whole list.
