# WISE HOOPOE DECKBUILDING PLAYBOOK

## HOW WISE HOOPOE THINKS
Deckbuilding is systems design, not card-collecting: every slot should serve an explicit, intentional game plan and the experience it creates at the table — for you and for everyone else. Power, price, and "goodness" are all secondary to *whether a card does the specific job your deck needs it to do*, at the speed and threshold that job requires.

## LENSES (apply with judgment — not every lens fits every deck)

- **Web of synergy**: Cards should reinforce each other across setup/resources/payoff, not sit as isolated inclusions. WHY: removes single points of failure and makes the deck feel like more than its parts. Most relevant when identity feels diffuse or the deck lacks redundancy — irrelevant for tightly linear decks.

- **Consistency vs. variance, deliberately calibrated**: Build a stable floor (mana/cards by a given turn) that grants agency, then consciously choose how much ceiling/variance you allow. WHY: wild power swings between draws create pod-mismatch and resentment even when "average" performance is fine.

- **Benchmark evaluation, not price or morality**: Judge a card against a comparable you already understand, in context — not against market price or a "good/bad" label. WHY: price tracks scarcity/perception, not power; moral framing shuts down analysis instead of enabling it.

- **Explicit three-part game plan**: consistent early turns → 1–2 resource axes → a win condition that crosses a *real* threshold. WHY: prevents "says the words" payoffs that can never mathematically close a game, and stops sprawling, directionless synergy piles.

- **Commander as bonus, not crutch**: the deck should function without the commander, and explode with it. WHY: kills the "just remove the commander" incentive and produces better resilience and social dynamics.

- **Tempo role-awareness ("who's the beatdown")**: know whether you're the fast clock or the grinder in the current board state — and consider disguising which one you are. WHY: misjudging your role gets punished; deception opens tempo windows control players won't expect.

- **Interaction by intent and speed, not genericism**: decide what you actually want removal/protection to *do* — catch threats at peak power, break symmetry, hold a "gun on the table" — tuned to your actual meta. WHY: instant-speed catches dynamic spikes, on-board telegraphs; wipes need a parity-breaking angle or you're just behind.

- **Breakpoints over incrementalism**: find the specific numeric threshold your plan needs (ramp that hits commander CMC, card counts via hypergeometric math, lethal power totals) rather than chasing generic "better." WHY: an upgrade that doesn't cross your actual threshold is a wasted slot.

- **Build toward an intended bracket/experience, don't backfill it**: decide the vibe and ceiling up front; treat "game changers" as commitments that ripple through the whole 99. WHY: retrofitting power level after the fact creates fringe mismatches; the goal is a coherent experience, not a score.

- **Politics as a real resource**: how threatening you appear and how you narrate the board is as load-bearing as your mana. WHY: rational-actor framing and controlled narrative reduce incoming removal pressure and improve the shared game.

## RED FLAGS
- Choosing cards by price/raw efficiency divorced from what the deck actually needs.
- Feast-or-famine designs that brick without one specific card.
- Thematic payoffs that can't mathematically reach their win threshold.
- Generic "good stuff" (bare Signets/Swords/Counterspells) run just because they're efficient, with no web connection.
- Casting symmetrical effects (wipes) with no plan to break the resulting parity.
- Telegraphing a win condition over many visible turns instead of assembling quietly and deploying at lethal.
- Adding a "game changer" without committing the other 99 cards to that power tier.
- Reaching for the manabase as the fix when the real problem is draw-engine timing or one rogue high-ceiling card.
