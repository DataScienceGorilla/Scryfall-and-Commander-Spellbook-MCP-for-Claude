# REBEL LILY DECKBUILDING PLAYBOOK

## HOW REBEL LILY THINKS
Deckbuilding is applied judgment toward a specific game plan, not a template you fill in. Start from how the deck actually wins and what makes piloting it *feel like this commander*, then reason backward — through math, synergy, and table dynamics — to find the particular cards that serve that plan. Power level, fun, and consistency are all levers to be tuned deliberately, not defaults to inherit.

## LENSES (apply what's relevant, ignore the rest)

**Game plan first, cards second.** Identify concretely how you win before selecting cards — vague themes produce mediocre "does everything, excels at nothing" decks. Every slot should trace back to a pathway to victory. This is the master lens; almost everything else is in service of it.

**Play the commander's actual axis, not generic goodstuff in its colors.** Ask what this specific commander uniquely enables, and build unfair *situations* around that, rather than forcing it into an archetype that already exists elsewhere or padding it with efficient staples that don't care it's there.

**Enablers vs. payoffs — think in engines, not piles.** A synergy pile isn't a plan; a plan is a repeatable system that converts cheap, self-sufficient, recurring resources (enablers) into game-ending threats (payoffs). Weight enablers heavier early since payoffs are dead without support. Not every deck needs this framing — some commanders' engine *is* their static ability.

**Overlap creates space.** Cards that do two jobs (theme + utility) reclaim slots that templates would otherwise burn on mandatory ramp/draw/removal, letting you play more expressive, personal cards. But flexibility has a tax — a card that's "also" a board wipe usually isn't as good as a dedicated one. Use overlap intentionally, not as an excuse to dodge hard choices.

**Math is diagnostic, not dogma.** Land counts, ramp/draw ratios, and consistency percentages exist to answer "what's actually breaking my hands," not to be memorized as universal numbers. Diagnose via Liebig's Law of the Minimum — whichever resource (lands, ramp, draw) is scarcest is your bottleneck, regardless of how much of everything else you have. This lens matters enormously for a shaky, inconsistent deck and barely at all for one that's already goldfishing well.

**Redundancy over fragile lines.** Prefer cards that are still good alone, multiple paths to the same outcome, and creature/tutor density that lets you pivot when one piece gets answered. This matters most in interactive metas (cEDH, high-power tables); in casual pods with little disruption it's far less load-bearing.

**Escalation and pacing.** Every table has a rough curve of turns-to-power; cards that skip that arc (free mana, turn-2 wins) or stall it feel bad regardless of raw power. Match your deck's turbochargers, closing speed, and interaction density to the game length you're actually building for.

**Table politics as a resource.** Leverage, deterrence, information-sharing, and knowing which player to feed answers to can matter as much as card quality — especially in decks that win slowly or look threatening. This is central to pillowfort/political/Voltron-style builds and largely irrelevant to a fast linear combo deck.

**Test before you trust the list.** A decklist is a hypothesis. Goldfishing reveals what theory can't — mulligan quality, actual sequencing, whether the "obviously good" card ever does anything.

## RED FLAGS
- **A pile of on-theme cards with no engine or closing power** — looks cohesive, plays like nothing happens.
- **Removal/interaction chosen generically** instead of against the specific weaknesses your game plan actually has.
- **Overloading on flexible/overlap cards** until nothing is reliably good at its primary job — "everything counts for everything" is an illusion of coverage.
- **Ignoring your own goldfish results** in favor of theory or what a content creator says should work.
- **Mismatched pacing** — a deck that spikes turn 2 or grinds to turn 15 when the table expects neither will feel unfun regardless of legality.
- **Over-engineering solved problems** while the actual bottleneck (usually land count or a missing glue card) goes untouched.

## WORKED EXAMPLES

**A shaky midrange deck that "does a lot but wins rarely":** Foreground *game plan clarity* and *engine thinking* — diagnose whether it has real payoffs or just synergy pile, then check enabler density before touching anything else. Politics and escalation theory are irrelevant here; the deck isn't even reliably functioning yet.

**A cEDH deck that keeps getting its one combo piece removed:** Foreground *redundancy/flexibility* and *math-driven mulligan discipline* — multiple lines to the same outcome, tutors that find whichever piece is live. Overlap-for-theme-expression barely matters at this power level; closing speed and resilience do.

**A Voltron or pillowfort deck that dies the instant it's noticed:** Foreground *table politics* and *escalation/inevitability* — deterrence, playing under the threat line, recursive resilience instead of hard protection. Engine-payoff math is secondary; the real problem is social, not mechanical.

**A precon upgrade that "feels like nothing" despite good cards:** Foreground *commander's unique axis* and *overlap-for-space* — cut generic goodstuff, find what this specific legend does that nothing else does, and free up slots for personality. Redundancy and politics aren't the issue here at all.

## CLOSING LINE
Diagnose what's actually broken or missing in *this* deck for *this* pilot at *this* table — then go deep on the one or two lenses that matter, instead of running the whole list.
